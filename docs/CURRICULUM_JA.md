# Pontino — Japanese curriculum map (DRAFT for approval)

Status: **Phase 0 draft. Nothing here is built yet.** Unit titles, order and counts are proposals.

References used for scope and sequence only (no content copied): JF Standard can-do statements, Irodori (Starter → Pre-Intermediate) topic order, Marugoto topic/culture pairing, JLPT N5–N2 lists as a measurable checklist. Working map: A1 ≈ N5, A2 ≈ N4, B1 ≈ N3, B2 ≈ N2. This mapping is approximate: JLPT does not test speaking or writing, and CEFR does not define kanji counts.

## 1. The honest time budget

| Stage | Typical guided hours (kanji-free background) | At 15 min/day (~91 h/year) |
|---|---|---|
| Now → trip (24 weeks) | — | **~42 h** available |
| A1 complete (≈N5) | 150–300 h total, you have a head start in vocabulary | finished ~3–5 months after the trip |
| A2 (≈N4) | +250–350 h | ~3 years |
| B1 (≈N3) | +400–600 h | several more years |
| B2 (≈N2) | 1,600–2,800 h cumulative | **not reachable at 15 min/day alone** |

What this means in practice:
- **Survival Japanese by May 2027 is realistic** in 42 focused hours, because you already have vocabulary. It means: handle the scenarios below politely, read common signs, catch the usual staff questions. It does not mean free conversation.
- B1 and B2 need far more input than a 15-minute lesson gives. The app will be honest about it: from A2 onward it pushes outside reading and listening (NHK NEWS WEB EASY, podcasts, sports broadcasts) and counts weekend deep dives.
- I will write detailed content only as far as you are actually going to use it soon. B1/B2 below are outlines.

## 2. Step 0 — Diagnostic (one session, ~15 min)

| Part | What it checks | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Kana | Hiragana + katakana recognition, a few production items | Pass → kana bootcamp is skipped, romaji stays off |
| Vocabulary sweep | ~120 N5 words in quick "know it / don't" rounds, with spot checks | Known words enter SRS as *known* with long intervals, not taught again |
| Particles | は が を に で へ と も の in short sentences | Sets how fast A1-01/02 move |
| Verb forms | ます / ません / ました, て-form, ない-form recognition | Decides whether A1-05/06 can be compressed |

**Kana bootcamp** (only if needed): 4 short units — hiragana 1, hiragana 2 (dakuten, combos, small っ), katakana 1, katakana 2 (long vowels, loanword sounds).

## 3. Pre-trip plan — 24 weeks, 16 Nov 2026 → early May 2027

**Proposed shape (decision D3 in the plan): 18 Trip units + 6 A1 structure units, one unit per week.**
The spec asked for ~24 Trip units *plus* A1 core alongside. At 15 minutes a day there is room for one unit per week, so 24 slots total. Since your gap is structure, I propose that 6 of those slots are pure structure units, placed just before the Trip units that need them. Every Trip unit still carries one structure focus, and every structure unit uses travel/sports/geography sentences.

Weekly rhythm: Mon–Fri one 15-minute lesson each · Sat unit review + can-do check · Sun optional deep dive (Japan Atlas).

| 24 | **T18** Trip rehearsal | A full day in Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka | Get through simulated days end to end · "Ready for Japan" checkpoint | Review of everything; no new grammar | Mixed | **Golden Week note** (see below) |

**Golden Week 2027:** 29 Apr (Shōwa Day), 3–5 May (Constitution Day, Greenery Day, Children's Day). If the trip touches these dates the app will flag crowds, sold-out Shinkansen reserved seats and the need to book ahead. `factCheck`: confirm dates and any substitute holidays before publishing.

**Kanji in Trip Mode:** about 120 sign/menu/station kanji, taught inside words first (出口 before 出 and 口), with KanjiVG stroke order. After the trip the order switches to JLPT lists.

**"Ready for Japan" score** = share of the Trip can-do goals above (about 60) that you have passed in a unit review and kept alive in SRS.

## 8. Japan Atlas

Runs as the Sunday deep dive and feeds the stamp book. No fixed itinerary (decided 2026-11-16): the Atlas works for any Japan trip. Order: the 8 regions, the neighbourhoods most first-time visitors pass through (Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka), then all 47 prefectures. You can pin any prefecture to move it to the front. Each entry: 8–12 words, a short graded text, one history or geography fact with a `factCheck` flag, one etiquette or practical tip, one stamp.
